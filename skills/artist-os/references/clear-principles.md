# Clear writing principles

Use these principles only when the Text Generation Plan says clarity and concision serve the form.

## One paragraph, one job

Each paragraph or block should do one clear job. If it does two jobs, split it or choose the stronger one.

## Put the point where the reader can find it

Lead with the sentence that explains why the paragraph exists, unless suspense or poetic delay is part of the approved form.

## Use active voice when it helps

Active voice usually improves force:

> The speaker refuses the door.

not:

> The door is refused by the speaker.

Passive voice is acceptable when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally hidden.

## Use concrete language

Prefer specific images, actions, stakes, examples, and constraints over abstract labels.

## Omit needless words

Cut filler that does not change meaning, rhythm, or tone.

Common cuts:

- in order to -> to,
- due to the fact that -> because,
- at this point in time -> now,
- in the event that -> if,
- has the ability to -> can.

## Keep related words together

Do not separate subject, verb, and object with clutter unless the form deliberately delays meaning.

## Preserve emphasis

Put the strongest word, image, or turn where the sentence lands.

## Keep useful texture

Clear does not mean plain. Preserve voice, musicality, tension, and strangeness when they serve the approved text.
