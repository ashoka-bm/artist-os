# Fichtean Curve

**Entry id**: `fichtean_curve`

**Scope**: A crisis-led story begins in pressure and escalates through repeated crises toward climax and aftermath.

**Structure grounding**: Fichtean Curve / crisis-escalation fiction craft tradition.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The work should start in trouble, reveal context through action, and sustain momentum through escalating crises rather than extended setup. Crisis may be emotional, perceptual, relational, custodial, or external; it does not have to mean action-thriller danger.

**Core movement**: The work begins inside active pressure. Each crisis forces action, reveals missing context, and raises cost. The accumulating crises crest in a climax, then resolve into consequence, changed state, or survival residue.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate crisis | Drops the audience into active pressure. |
| Context through action | Reveals necessary backstory only as pressure demands it. |
| Escalating crisis | Forces a new choice, loss, discovery, or narrowing path. |
| Reversal crisis | Changes what the pressure means or who controls it. |
| Climactic crisis | Forces the decisive confrontation, choice, escape, or revelation. |
| Falling consequence | Shows the cost, altered condition, and remaining residue. |

**Key turns**:

- Immediate crisis: the work begins with motion instead of explanation.
- Escalating crisis: each pressure point changes the available choices.
- Reversal crisis: the audience's understanding of danger shifts.
- Climactic crisis: accumulated pressure reaches its decisive test.

**Compression guidance**: For short works, use one opening crisis, one recontextualizing escalation, and one decisive crisis. Let aftermath be a single image, sentence, sound, or changed relation.

**Expansion guidance**: For long works, vary crisis types so escalation does not become repetitive. Track what each crisis reveals, costs, and closes off. Use Long-Work Stewardship to track causal escalation across parts.

**Medium affinities**: Thrillers, mysteries, action, horror, suspense, survival stories, crisis-led essays, intense video sequences, and sound or image series that should feel like accumulating pressure.

**Common failure modes**:

- The work starts fast but without meaningful stakes.
- Crises repeat intensity instead of changing choice or knowledge.
- Backstory arrives as explanation rather than pressure-triggered revelation.
- The climax is only the biggest event, not the decisive pressure.
- Aftermath disappears, leaving no emotional consequence.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What active pressure opens the work?
- What context can be delayed until pressure requires it?
- How does each crisis change what is possible?
- What crisis reverses the audience's understanding?
- What decisive test does the escalation demand?
- What residue remains after survival or resolution?
