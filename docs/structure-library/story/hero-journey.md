# Hero's Journey

**Entry id**: `hero_journey`

**Scope**: A figure leaves an ordinary orientation, crosses into trial or mystery, gains a boon or revelation, and returns changed.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `canonical_framework`

**Use when**: The work centers on threshold crossing, ordeal, transformation, return, initiation, identity change, mythic scale, or a gift brought back to a community.

**Core movement**: The protagonist or speaker begins inside a known world. A call, lack, wound, or summons pulls them across a threshold. Trials strip away the old self. An ordeal or revelation creates a boon. Return tests whether that boon can enter ordinary life.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary world | Establishes what feels known, limited, safe, or incomplete. |
| Call | Pulls the figure toward the unknown. |
| Refusal or hesitation | Shows the cost of crossing. |
| Threshold | Marks entry into the transformed field. |
| Helper or ally | Gives aid without replacing the figure's trial. |
| Trials | Test the old self and reveal new capacities. |
| Ordeal | Forces symbolic death, descent, exposure, or irreversible change. |
| Boon | Names what has been gained, stolen, learned, or recovered. |
| Return threshold | Tests whether the changed figure can re-enter ordinary life. |
| Gift or residue | Shows what the journey gives, costs, or fails to restore. |

**Key turns**:

- Call: the ordinary world no longer holds the whole truth.
- Threshold: the figure enters a field with different rules.
- Ordeal: the old identity cannot solve the central pressure.
- Boon: the journey yields power, knowledge, release, or burden.
- Return: transformation must meet the world it left.

**Compression guidance**: In short works, keep only the ordinary state, threshold, ordeal, and return residue. The helper, trials, and boon can appear as implications rather than separate Beats.

**Expansion guidance**: In long works, trials can become sequences, chapters, scenes, quests, or movements. Avoid treating every possible stage as required. Let the chosen ordeal and return logic decide which stages matter.

**Medium affinities**: Mythic fiction, memoir-shaped essays, transformation narratives, films, games, ritual or performance pieces, concept albums, and visual sequences built around departure, descent, and return.

**Common failure modes**:

- The entry forces every possible stage into the work.
- The structure becomes generic self-improvement instead of real transformation.
- The helper solves the journey for the protagonist.
- The boon is private victory with no return pressure.
- The structure imports a heroic savior frame the Artist Meaning does not support.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What ordinary world is being left?
- What makes the threshold emotionally irreversible?
- What must the old self lose?
- What is the boon, and who is it for?
- Does return restore, change, indict, or fail the ordinary world?
