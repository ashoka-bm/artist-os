# Dan Harmon Story Circle

**Entry id**: `dan_harmon_story_circle`

**Scope**: A character or speaker leaves a familiar state to pursue a need, pays a price, returns, and changes.

**Structure grounding**: Dan Harmon / Story Circle episodic and screenwriting craft framework.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The work needs a compact cyclical change arc, especially for episodes, short fiction, comedy, animation, reflective pieces, or character-driven transformation.

**Core movement**: A person, voice, or argument begins in a familiar condition, wants or needs something, enters an unfamiliar pressure, adapts, obtains or confronts the desired thing, pays a cost, returns toward familiar ground, and is changed by the loop.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Familiar state | Establishes the starting world, identity, habit, or assumption. |
| Need | Reveals what is missing, desired, denied, or unstable. |
| Threshold | Moves the work into unfamiliar pressure. |
| Search | Forces adaptation, experimentation, or misread attempts. |
| Find | Gives access to the desired object, truth, status, person, or answer. |
| Cost | Shows what the finding demands or damages. |
| Return | Brings the work back toward its starting world, role, or audience. |
| Change | Shows the altered self, relation, perception, or condition. |

**Key turns**:

- Need: familiar life is not sufficient.
- Threshold: the work enters an unfamiliar zone.
- Find: the desired thing is reached or exposed.
- Cost: acquisition creates consequence.
- Change: the return proves transformation.

**Compression guidance**: For compact works, merge familiar state and need into one opening. Let threshold/search/find form the middle. Let cost, return, and change compress into a final turn or image.

**Expansion guidance**: For episodes or chapters, repeat the circle at different scales while preserving cumulative change. Use Long-Work Stewardship to prevent each loop from resetting the character or argument without consequence.

**Medium affinities**: Episodic television, short films, animation, comedy, short fiction, personal essays, songs with a return, and image or sound sequences built around departure and changed return.

**Common failure modes**:

- The circle becomes a disguised Hero's Journey when a simpler loop would do.
- The need is vague, so the threshold has no pressure.
- The find beat gives success without cost.
- The return resets the work instead of revealing change.
- The change is stated rather than dramatized or embodied.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What familiar condition begins the loop?
- What does the protagonist, speaker, or argument need?
- What unfamiliar pressure forces adaptation?
- What is found, obtained, or confronted?
- What price does the finding create?
- How does the return prove change?
