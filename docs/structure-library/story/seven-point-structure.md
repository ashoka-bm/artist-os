# Seven-Point Structure

**Entry id**: `seven_point_structure`

**Scope**: A plot-driven story moves from hook to resolution through turns, pinch points, midpoint reversal, and climax.

**Structure grounding**: Seven-point plot structure used in genre fiction and screenwriting craft.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The work needs clear genre momentum, alternating pressure and decision points, a strong midpoint reversal, and visible movement from initial state to final state.

**Core movement**: The story opens with a hook, commits through a first turn, faces external pressure, reverses at the midpoint, endures stronger pressure, commits through a final turn into a climactic test, and resolves in a state that meaningfully contrasts the opening.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Establishes the audience question, initial condition, and reason to continue. |
| First turn | Pushes the work from setup into the central conflict or pursuit. |
| First pinch | Applies antagonistic, systemic, or internal pressure. |
| Midpoint reversal | Changes the protagonist's, speaker's, argument's, or central force's understanding, agency, or strategy. |
| Second pinch | Intensifies pressure and shows the cost of failure. |
| Final turn | Commits the work to the climactic test, final answer, or strategy. |
| Resolution | Shows the outcome of the climax and changed condition. |

**Key turns**:

- First turn: the main story path begins.
- First pinch: opposition becomes concrete.
- Midpoint reversal: the work changes from reaction to new understanding or agency.
- Second pinch: failure becomes newly possible or newly costly.
- Final turn: the ending path becomes unavoidable.

**Compression guidance**: In short works, combine hook and first turn. Use one pinch rather than two if the pressure is simple. Let midpoint reversal and final turn collapse when the work is built around one decisive reversal or strategic commitment. If the governing movement is primarily problem to reframe to return, use `problem_reframe_return` instead.

**Expansion guidance**: In long works, use pinch points to reveal different faces of opposition. Let midpoint reversal change strategy, not only stakes. Track cause-and-effect escalation through Long-Work Stewardship.

**Medium affinities**: Genre fiction, thrillers, mysteries, adventure, screenplays, serialized fiction, games, and narrative essays that need strong plot-turn clarity.

**Common failure modes**:

- Pinch points repeat the same pressure without escalation.
- The midpoint reverses information but not agency.
- The final turn feels like a plan recap instead of a commitment.
- The resolution solves plot without resolving the opening condition.
- The structure over-privileges plot mechanics over Artist Meaning.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What hook creates the audience's first question?
- What turn starts the real path?
- What pressure proves the opposition or cost?
- What midpoint changes understanding or agency?
- What second pressure makes failure concrete?
- What final turn commits the ending?
