# Save The Cat Beat Sheet

**Entry id**: `save_the_cat_beat_sheet`

**Scope**: A commercial story moves through a granular beat sequence that aligns external plot pressure with internal change.

**Structure grounding**: Blake Snyder / Save the Cat commercial screenwriting beat-sheet tradition.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The work needs tight commercial pacing, visible turns, a clear midpoint, a low point, a decisive final movement, and strong contrast between beginning and ending state.

**Core movement**: The work opens with a visible initial condition, disturbs it through a catalytic event, commits to a new story world, tests the premise through rising complication, breaks the false solution, reaches loss or collapse, then synthesizes the lesson into a final action and changed image.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Opening image beat | Shows the initial state, imbalance, or promise in concrete form; this is a story-state beat, not a required visual asset. |
| Theme pressure | Names or implies the human question the story will test. |
| Setup | Establishes world, desire, flaw, stakes, and missing piece. |
| Catalyst | Breaks the stable condition and creates the story's demand. |
| Debate | Lets hesitation, refusal, or uncertainty reveal the cost of entering. |
| Break into new world | Commits the protagonist or argument to the main path. |
| Promise of the premise | Explores the central situation's appeal, tension, or pattern. |
| Midpoint | Raises stakes, shifts direction, or creates a false victory or false defeat. |
| Bad pressure closes in | External and internal forces narrow the path. |
| Loss point | The old approach fails and the work reaches maximum doubt. |
| New synthesis | The work discovers what must change to answer the pressure. |
| Final movement | Converts the synthesis into action, proof, or decisive articulation. |
| Final image beat | Shows the changed state in contrast with the opening; this is a story-state beat, not a required visual asset. |

**Key turns**:

- Catalyst: the prior state is no longer enough.
- Break into new world: the work commits to the premise.
- Midpoint: the story's direction or stakes change.
- Loss point: the false solution collapses.
- Final image beat: the audience sees the transformation.

**Compression guidance**: In short works, combine setup, theme pressure, catalyst, and debate into one opening movement. Combine promise of the premise, midpoint, and bad pressure into one central turn. Let final movement and final image share a compressed close.

**Expansion guidance**: In long works, use the beat sheet as a pacing scaffold, not a formula. Let subplots pressure theme and flaw. Use Long-Work Stewardship to track midpoint consequence, low-point causality, and opening/final state contrast.

**Medium affinities**: Feature scripts, commercial fiction, memoir with a strong transformation arc, serialized video, pitch-oriented story development, and long-form text that needs clear beat visibility.

**Common failure modes**:

- The beat sheet becomes visible machinery.
- Beats arrive on schedule but do not arise from Artist Meaning.
- The midpoint changes plot facts without changing pressure.
- The low point feels inserted rather than earned.
- The final image signals change the work has not actually created.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What opening image captures the starting imbalance?
- What human question is being tested?
- What catalytic demand cannot be ignored?
- Where does the premise show its fullest promise?
- What false solution must fail before the final movement?
- What final image beat proves change without over-explaining it?
