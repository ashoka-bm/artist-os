# Three-Act Structure

**Entry id**: `three_act_structure`

**Scope**: A narrative or argument moves from setup, through escalating confrontation, into resolution.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The work needs a clear promise, a central dramatic question, rising pressure, a decisive climax, and a changed final state.

**Core movement**: A stable or legible world is disrupted. The protagonist, speaker, or argument commits to a path, faces worsening pressure, reaches a decisive test, and lands in a changed condition.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Makes the audience care before the rules are fully explained. |
| Setup | Establishes world, stakes, voice, and central pressure. |
| Inciting incident | Disturbs the prior state and creates the main question. |
| First major choice | Commits the work to the central path. |
| Escalation | Complicates the path and raises cost. |
| Midpoint turn | Reframes what the work is really about. |
| Consequences | Shows the cost of the midpoint turn. |
| Darkest point | Tests whether the desired change can still happen. |
| Final plan | Focuses the remaining action or argument. |
| Climax | Answers the central question through action, choice, revelation, or refusal. |
| Resolution | Shows the changed state and remaining residue. |

**Key turns**:

- Inciting incident: the old state cannot simply continue.
- First major choice: the work accepts its central path.
- Midpoint turn: the audience's understanding changes.
- Darkest point: the work reaches maximum doubt, cost, or contradiction.
- Climax: the main question receives its strongest answer.

**Compression guidance**: In short works, combine setup, inciting incident, and first major choice into one opening move. Combine escalation and midpoint into one turn. Let the climax and resolution share a final beat when the ending needs residue more than explanation.

**Expansion guidance**: In long works, divide Act 2 into escalation and consequence halves. Let subplots echo, pressure, or invert the central question. Use Long-Work Stewardship to track open threads, character pressure, reversals, and payoff timing.

**Medium affinities**: Feature scripts, narrative fiction, long video, essays with a decisive argument arc, games with a central transformation, and image or sound series that stage a clear before-pressure-after movement.

**Common failure modes**:

- The structure becomes a checklist instead of a pressure system.
- Act 2 repeats obstacles without changing the question.
- The midpoint adds information but does not reframe the work.
- The climax is loud but does not answer the central pressure.
- The resolution explains meaning the work has not earned.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What question does the audience need answered by the climax?
- What old state is disrupted?
- What choice makes return impossible?
- What turn changes the audience's understanding?
- What residue should remain after resolution?
