# Frame Story / Nested Return

**Entry id**: `frame_story_nested_return`

**Scope**: A framing situation opens a nested story, memory, document, testimony, or tale, then returns with changed meaning.

**Structure grounding**: Frame narrative and nested-story literary craft tradition.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The work depends on a narrator, listener, archive, interview, memory, found document, testimony, or story-within-story relationship. Object, image, or archive testimony can count as nested material only when the return frame changes meaning.

**Core movement**: A framing situation creates the reason a story is being told or found. The nested material carries the main pressure. The return to the frame changes the frame's meaning, reveals the act of telling as consequential, or makes the audience question authority, memory, distance, or inheritance.

**Typical Beat Roles**:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Frame situation | Establishes who is telling, finding, hearing, or preserving the story. |
| Telling trigger | Explains why the nested material must be entered now. |
| Nested entry | Moves into memory, document, tale, testimony, or embedded narrative. |
| Nested pressure | Carries the main emotional, symbolic, or narrative movement. |
| Frame disturbance | Lets the nested story affect the frame's certainty, relation, or stakes. |
| Return | Comes back to the frame with changed meaning or consequence. |
| Residue | Leaves a final question, inheritance, warning, proof, or altered relation. |

**Key turns**:

- Telling trigger: the frame creates a need for the nested story.
- Nested entry: authority shifts into the embedded material.
- Frame disturbance: the nested story changes the frame's stability.
- Return: the act of telling or receiving becomes consequential.

**Compression guidance**: In short works, keep the frame minimal: one reason to tell, one nested movement, one return. Do not add a frame unless the return changes the meaning.

**Expansion guidance**: In long works, track layers of narrator, document, memory, and audience knowledge. Use Long-Work Stewardship to prevent nested material from losing its relationship to the frame.

**Medium affinities**: Works where testimony, memory, archival relation, or nested telling changes the return frame, including memoir, literary fiction, documentary, interview-based work, archival work, letter-based work, found-footage-style video, concept albums, installations, and long narrative projects. Use Cultural Format Structure separately when the accepted output shape has its own audience-facing grammar.

**Common failure modes**:

- The frame is decorative and does not change the return, the frame's meaning, or the audience's understanding of the nested story.
- The nested story overwhelms the reason for telling.
- The return repeats the opening instead of transforming it.
- Narrator authority is unclear by accident.
- Too many layers blur rather than deepen meaning.

**Adaptation questions**:

- Who or what frames the story?
- Why is the nested material entered now?
- What does the nested story know that the frame does not?
- How does the nested story disturb the frame?
- What changes when the work returns?
- What residue should the frame hold after the return?
