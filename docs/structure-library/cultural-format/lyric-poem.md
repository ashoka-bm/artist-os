# Lyric Poem

**Entry id**: `lyric_poem`

**Scope**: A compact poem concentrates perception, feeling, image, sound, and turn into a non-narrative or lightly narrative emotional event.

**Structure grounding**: Lyric poetry craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape is a poem whose primary grammar is image, voice, feeling, thought, sound, compression, and resonance rather than plot.

**Audience promise**: The reader will experience a concentrated shift in perception or feeling that could not be delivered as prose explanation.

**Audience Hook logic**: Begin with image, address, sound, paradox, question, claim, or pressure that opens a field of feeling quickly.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening image / pressure | Establishes the poem's field of attention. | Required |
| Speaker or stance | Gives the poem a position, even if unnamed. | Required |
| Image / sound development | Builds association, tension, rhythm, or pattern. | Required |
| Volta or shift | Turns perception, logic, address, scale, or feeling. | Required |
| Compression / selection | Removes explanatory filler so each line carries weight. | Required |
| Final resonance | Leaves an afterimage, echo, question, or changed relation. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The poem pays off through a shift in perception, not a solved argument. The final movement should make the opening field feel altered.

**Pacing norms**:

- Prefer charged particulars over general feeling labels.
- Let line breaks, sound, and syntax shape attention.
- Compress explanation unless plainness is the chosen force.
- Do not over-explain the final resonance.

**Required decisions**:

- What is the poem's central field of image, thought, or feeling?
- Who or what is being addressed, if anyone?
- Where does the poem turn?
- What should lineation or prose-poem shape do?
- What must be left unsaid for resonance?

**Common failure modes**:

- The poem states feelings without embodying them.
- Images accumulate without a turn or pressure.
- The ending explains the poem.
- Line breaks are decorative rather than structural.
- Abstract language replaces sensory or rhythmic force.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What image or sound pattern can hold the emotional payload?
- Where should the poem change scale, address, logic, or perception?
- Which words can be removed without weakening the force?
- What final image should keep vibrating?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `image_field`
- `lineation_notes`
- `voice_notes`
