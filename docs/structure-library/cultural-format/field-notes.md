# Field Notes

**Entry id**: `field_notes`

**Scope**: A reflective observational piece gathers direct impressions, fragments, scenes, or lessons from lived practice or close attention.

**Structure grounding**: Field note, notebook, dispatch, and reflective essay pattern.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `platform_pattern_evidence`

**Use when**: The piece should feel observed and provisional rather than fully argued, reported, or systematized.

**Audience promise**: The reader will get honest observations from the field and a useful pattern or question emerging from them.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with a concrete observation, scene, repeated detail, or unresolved question that signals firsthand attention.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation frame | Establishes where the notes come from. | Required |
| Field details | Presents scenes, fragments, quotes, behaviors, or textures. | Required |
| Pattern noticed | Names what seems to be emerging. | Required |
| Provisional interpretation | Offers meaning without overclaiming. | Required |
| Open question or takeaway | Leaves the reader with a useful thought, not a sealed thesis. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff is sharpened attention: the reader sees a pattern while understanding it may still be unfolding.

**Pacing norms**:

- Lead with observed detail.
- Let fragments accumulate before naming the pattern.
- Keep claims modest when evidence is local.
- Preserve texture instead of flattening everything into advice.

**Required decisions**:

- What field, setting, or practice produced the notes?
- Which details are specific enough to earn trust?
- What pattern can be responsibly named?
- What remains unresolved?

**Common failure modes**:

- The notes become vague reflection without observed material.
- The piece overclaims from a small sample.
- Fragments never cohere.
- The ending turns into generic advice.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What did the writer actually see, hear, or experience?
- What detail keeps returning?
- What can be inferred, and what cannot?
- What question should stay open?
