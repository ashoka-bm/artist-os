# Speech

**Entry id**: `speech`

**Scope**: A spoken address moves a specific audience through occasion, connection, claim, proof, and memorable close.

**Structure grounding**: Public speaking and rhetorical address convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The output is meant to be delivered aloud to an audience with a shared occasion, decision, grief, celebration, or call.

**Audience promise**: The audience will feel addressed, oriented, moved, and given a clear meaning or next step.

**Audience Hook logic**: Begin with the occasion, a shared truth, story, image, question, or direct address that creates attention in the room.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occasion | Names why this address is happening now. | Required |
| Audience bond | Shows the speaker understands the room. | Required |
| Central claim | Gives the speech a governing idea. | Required |
| Proof or story | Builds trust through evidence, memory, example, or narrative. | Required |
| Crescendo or turn | Raises stakes, emotion, or clarity. | Required |
| Close or call | Lands in action, blessing, warning, resolve, or shared image. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff should make the audience feel the occasion differently and know what posture or action follows.

**Pacing norms**:

- Write for breath and memory.
- Repeat key language with purpose.
- Build toward a spoken close.
- Avoid dense paragraph logic that cannot be heard.

**Required decisions**:

- Who is in the room?
- What occasion requires speech rather than article prose?
- What does the audience need to feel or do?
- What repeated phrase, image, or rhythm should carry memory?

**Common failure modes**:

- The speech reads like an essay.
- The audience is generic.
- The close summarizes instead of landing.
- The rhetoric intensifies without concrete meaning.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What does this audience already know, fear, or hope?
- What authority does the speaker have in this room?
- Where should the speech turn emotionally?
- What line should remain after hearing it?
