# Relationship Arc

**Entry id**: `relationship_arc`

**Scope**: A long-text planning or review structure for how a relationship changes across scenes, chapters, or sequences.

**Structure grounding**: Relationship dynamics, pressure tests, rupture, repair, and new-equilibrium craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: A relationship is structurally important enough to require continuity across multiple parts.

**Authority mode**: Stewardship View first; CFS when delivered as a relationship planning document. Internally, prefer `relationship_continuity_view`.

**Audience promise**: The relationship will evolve through pressure rather than arbitrary mood changes.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with the charged dynamic: attraction, rivalry, dependence, resentment, loyalty, secrecy, obligation, power imbalance, or loss.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial dynamic | Defines the relationship state when the arc begins. | Required |
| Desire and resistance | Names what each side wants, fears, offers, or withholds. | Required |
| Pressure tests | Projects scenes or chapters that stress the bond. | Required |
| Rupture or shift | Identifies the decisive change in trust, power, intimacy, or knowledge. | Required |
| New equilibrium | States the relationship state after the change. | Required |
| Continuity constraints | Names rules or proposed updates affecting the relationship. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The arc pays off when the relationship reaches a new equilibrium that changes later choices, stakes, or self-understanding.

**Pacing norms**:

- Let relationship change occur through choices and consequences.
- Avoid repeating the same conflict without new information or cost.
- Track power and knowledge shifts explicitly.
- Keep relationship turns aligned with chapter or scene turns.
- Treat relationship changes as planned or proposed until evidence and approval make them active continuity.

**Required decisions**:

- Which relationship changes are planned versus draft-discovered?
- Which parts prove the shift?
- What relationship continuity rule applies?
- What unresolved tension should remain active?

**Common failure modes**:

- The relationship changes only because the outline says it does.
- The same argument repeats across chapters.
- A rupture happens without prior pressure tests.
- Draft discoveries become canon without approval.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What is the relationship's opening imbalance?
- What choice proves the shift?
- What cost does the new equilibrium create?
- Which continuity update would require story approval?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `relationship_map`
- `character_continuity_view`
- `open_thread_list`
