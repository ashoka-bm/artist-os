# Hook-Driven Article

**Entry id**: `hook_driven_article`

**Scope**: A reader-facing article opens with a concrete curiosity, tension, image, question, or promise, then converts that hook into a clear reason to keep reading.

**Structure grounding**: Article lede, nut graf, turn, and payoff craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The piece needs to earn attention quickly, then reward that attention with explanation, reflection, reporting, argument, or changed perception.

**Audience promise**: The opening hook will become meaningful. The article will not merely tease; it will reveal why the opening detail matters.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with a specific doorway the reader can enter: an image, scene, problem, claim, question, contradiction, surprising fact, or felt pressure. The hook should imply a question the article can answer or reframe.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Creates immediate curiosity, tension, identification, beauty, surprise, or promise. | Required |
| Context bridge | Moves from the hook into the article's real subject. | Mergeable |
| Promise / nut | Tells the reader what kind of meaning, question, or payoff the piece will deliver. | Required |
| Development | Builds the promised meaning through evidence, examples, scenes, reasoning, or reflection. | Required |
| Turn | Deepens, complicates, or reframes the first promise. | Required |
| Payoff / close | Returns to the hook, answers the implied question, or changes what the reader notices. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff should make the hook feel earned. It can answer the opening question, reverse it, widen it, or return to it with changed attention.

**Pacing norms**:

- Give the hook enough space to work before explaining it.
- Deliver the promise early enough that the reader knows why the article exists.
- Keep the middle from becoming a list of related material.
- Let the turn change the reader's expectation before the close.

**Required decisions**:

- What kind of hook is this: image, question, contradiction, problem, claim, scene, or surprising fact?
- What promise does the article make after the hook?
- What is the main turn?
- How does the close pay off the opening without merely repeating it?
- Which parts can merge without making the structure unclear?

**Common failure modes**:

- The hook is clickbait and does not represent the article.
- The promise arrives too late or never arrives.
- The middle accumulates points without movement.
- The turn restates the thesis instead of changing pressure.
- The ending explains the lesson instead of creating payoff.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What opening detail or pressure would make the reader continue?
- What does the reader think this is about at first?
- What does the article reveal it is really about?
- What must the reader understand, feel, or notice by the end?
- Should the close answer, invert, echo, or complicate the hook?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `section_outline`
- `promise_payoff`
- `open_threads` for long reported or essayistic pieces
