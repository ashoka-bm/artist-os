# Service Article / How-To

**Entry id**: `service_article_how_to`

**Scope**: A practical article helps the reader understand, choose, fix, do, or improve something.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape promises useful guidance, decision support, step-by-step instruction, or actionable explanation.

**Audience promise**: The reader will leave with a clearer path, better choice, safer action, or more usable understanding.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start from the reader's need, friction, risk, curiosity, or desired outcome. The hook should make the usefulness immediate without overstating the problem.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader problem or goal | Names the practical need the article serves. | Required |
| Promise of usefulness | Tells the reader what they will be able to understand or do. | Required |
| Scope and caveats | Defines who the advice is for and where it stops. | Required when risk, money, health, law, or safety matters |
| Steps or principles | Delivers the practical guidance in usable order. | Required |
| Examples or checks | Shows how the guidance applies in real situations. | Mergeable |
| Common mistakes | Prevents likely misuse or false confidence. | Mergeable |
| Takeaway / next action | Leaves the reader with a simple next move. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff is practical confidence. The reader should know what to do next and what not to overclaim.

**Pacing norms**:

- State usefulness early.
- Put the highest-value guidance before background detail.
- Break steps into scannable units.
- Keep caveats close to the advice they qualify.

**Required decisions**:

- What reader problem or goal is being served?
- Does the piece need steps, principles, checklist, decision tree, or explainer structure?
- What caveats protect the reader from misuse?
- What examples prove the advice is usable?
- What is the next action?

**Common failure modes**:

- The article promises help but delivers background only.
- The advice is too general to use.
- Caveats arrive after risky instructions.
- Steps hide assumptions the reader needs.
- The close repeats the intro instead of giving a next move.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What should the reader be able to do, decide, or understand afterward?
- What does the reader likely misunderstand before reading?
- What is the smallest useful sequence?
- What risk or boundary must be named?
- What one next move should end the piece?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `step_checklist`
- `decision_tree`
- `risk_caveat_map`
