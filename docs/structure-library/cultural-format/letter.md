# Letter

**Entry id**: `letter`

**Scope**: A written address from one sender to one or more recipients creates intimacy, purpose, and consequence through the act of writing.

**Structure grounding**: Epistolary and personal-letter craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape is a personal letter, fictional letter, epistolary fragment, farewell, apology, confession, appeal, or intimate written address.

**Audience promise**: The reader will feel the pressure of a message that exists because ordinary speech is unavailable, insufficient, unsafe, or too late.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with a clear addressee, occasion, emotional pressure, or withheld reason that makes the letter necessary.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Addressee and occasion | Establishes who is being written to and why now. | Required |
| Sender position | Reveals the writer's relationship, distance, guilt, desire, authority, or need. | Required |
| Stated purpose or pretext | Gives the letter a surface reason to exist. | Required |
| Disclosure / appeal / account | Carries the main emotional, narrative, or argumentative movement. | Required |
| Turn in relation | Changes what the letter asks, admits, refuses, releases, or understands. | Required |
| Closing gesture | Leaves an ask, blessing, wound, boundary, silence, or unresolved contact. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff is relational: the sender's position toward the recipient changes or becomes impossible to hide.

**Pacing norms**:

- Make the addressee felt even if silent.
- Let formality, intimacy, and omission reveal relationship.
- Use context only as needed to make the address meaningful.
- End with a gesture that belongs to the sender-recipient bond.

**Required decisions**:

- Who writes, and to whom?
- Why can this only be a letter?
- What is the stated purpose, and what is the deeper purpose?
- What does the sender risk by writing?
- What kind of closing would be honest to the relationship?

**Common failure modes**:

- The letter has no specific recipient pressure.
- It becomes an essay with "Dear..." attached.
- The sender explains the whole context without intimacy or risk.
- The ending resolves too neatly.
- The recipient's implied presence disappears.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What can the sender say in writing that they cannot say otherwise?
- What should the recipient understand, forgive, do, or carry?
- What remains unsent, crossed out, or implied?
- What closing line changes the emotional distance?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `relationship_arc`
- `character_brief`
- `open_threads` for epistolary sequences
