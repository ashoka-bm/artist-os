# List Article

**Entry id**: `list_article`

**Scope**: A reader-facing article organizes multiple items, reasons, lessons, examples, or recommendations into a purposeful sequence.

**Structure grounding**: List article promise, item progression, and synthesis convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `platform_pattern_evidence`

**Use when**: The piece's value depends on clear scanability, grouped ideas, ranked choices, examples, or a finite set of useful points.

**Audience promise**: The reader will receive a complete, ordered set whose logic is clear enough to scan and useful enough to read.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with the practical, emotional, surprising, or interpretive promise of the list, plus why this set belongs together.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List promise | States what the list gives the reader and why it matters. | Required |
| Selection logic | Explains how items were chosen, ranked, grouped, or bounded. | Required |
| Item sequence | Presents each item with enough substance to stand on its own. | Required |
| Progression | Orders items by usefulness, difficulty, chronology, contrast, or impact. | Required |
| Pattern / synthesis | Shows what the list reveals as a whole. | Required |
| Next action / close | Gives a final takeaway, choice, or changed frame. | Mergeable |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff should make the list feel curated rather than accumulated. The reader should see a pattern, priority, or decision more clearly by the end.

**Pacing norms**:

- Make each item quickly scannable.
- Avoid equal-weight monotony when items have different importance.
- Use parallel structure without making every item sound identical.
- Keep the close from becoming a second introduction.

**Required decisions**:

- What makes this a list rather than an essay or how-to?
- Is the order ranked, grouped, chronological, escalating, or arbitrary?
- What qualifies an item for inclusion?
- What pattern should the full set reveal?
- What should the reader do, choose, or notice after the list?

**Common failure modes**:

- The list is a loose pile of related points.
- Item headings promise more than the body delivers.
- The selection logic is hidden or weak.
- Items repeat the same function.
- The ending does not synthesize the set.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What promise makes the reader want the whole set?
- What would make the list feel complete?
- How should the sequence build?
- Which items can merge or be cut?
- What larger pattern does the set reveal?
