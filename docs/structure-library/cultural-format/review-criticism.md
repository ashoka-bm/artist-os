# Review / Criticism

**Entry id**: `review_criticism`

**Scope**: A critical piece evaluates a work, object, experience, event, or body of work through criteria, evidence, context, and judgment.

**Structure grounding**: Review and criticism craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The piece should help readers understand what something is, how it works, what it attempts, where it succeeds or fails, and why that judgment matters.

**Audience promise**: The reader will receive a clear, accountable judgment supported by evidence and framed by appropriate expectations.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with the strongest critical tension: a surprising judgment, vivid encounter, expectation gap, cultural stakes, or defining feature.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical hook | Establishes the central judgment, tension, or encounter. | Required |
| Object and context | Identifies what is being reviewed and the relevant frame. | Required |
| Criteria | Makes the standards of evaluation legible. | Required |
| Evidence / close reading | Supports judgment with details, scenes, features, or examples. | Required |
| Counterweight | Acknowledges limits, tradeoffs, alternate readings, or audience fit. | Required |
| Judgment | States what the critic concludes and why. | Required |
| Recommendation / final read | Clarifies who it is for, what remains, or what the work reveals. | Mergeable |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The turn often revises the initial expectation: the work is better, weaker, stranger, narrower, or more revealing than it first appears.

**Pacing norms**:

- Identify the object quickly.
- Avoid pure summary before evaluation.
- Move between claim and evidence.
- Let judgment sharpen as criteria become clear.

**Required decisions**:

- What is being evaluated?
- What criteria are fair for this object or genre?
- What evidence best supports the judgment?
- What audience or use case changes the judgment?
- Does the ending recommend, warn, contextualize, or interpret?

**Common failure modes**:

- The piece summarizes instead of judges.
- The judgment is strong but unsupported.
- Criteria are hidden or unfair.
- Personal taste is disguised as universal standard.
- The close gives a rating without meaning.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What expectation does the object create?
- What standard should it be held to?
- Which detail proves the judgment?
- What would a reasonable opposing reader say?
- What should the reader do or understand after the review?
