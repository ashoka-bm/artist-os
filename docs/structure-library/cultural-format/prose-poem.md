# Prose Poem

**Entry id**: `prose_poem`

**Scope**: A compact block of prose uses poetic compression, image logic, rhythm, and turn without line-break structure.

**Structure grounding**: Prose poetry craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape should read as poetry in prose form: condensed, image-driven, rhythmically alert, and resistant to ordinary essay or story explanation.

**Audience promise**: The reader will encounter a charged prose object whose compression, image logic, and turn create poetic resonance.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with a charged sentence, image, premise, contradiction, address, or impossible logic that establishes the poem's pressure immediately.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charged opening sentence | Creates immediate poetic pressure. | Required |
| Image / thought field | Establishes the associative terrain. | Required |
| Rhythmic prose movement | Uses syntax, cadence, and sentence pressure as structure. | Required |
| Leap, turn, or intensification | Changes the logic, scale, feeling, or perception. | Required |
| Compression discipline | Keeps the block dense and intentional. | Required |
| Final sentence / afterimage | Lands resonance without line-break closure. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff often comes through an associative leap, sharpened image, altered premise, or final sentence that changes the whole block's charge.

**Pacing norms**:

- Keep the piece compact unless the form deliberately accumulates.
- Use sentence rhythm instead of line breaks to control attention.
- Allow strangeness, but maintain felt coherence.
- Avoid ordinary paragraph explanation.

**Required decisions**:

- Why should this be prose-shaped rather than lineated?
- What image, premise, or logic holds the block together?
- Where does the block leap or turn?
- What sentence rhythm should govern the movement?
- What final sentence leaves resonance?

**Common failure modes**:

- The piece becomes a short paragraph with poetic adjectives.
- The logic is private without enough felt coherence.
- The prose explains instead of compressing.
- There is no turn, leap, or intensification.
- The final sentence closes too neatly.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What can sentence structure do that line breaks would otherwise do?
- What associative leap makes the form necessary?
- Which explanatory phrases can be removed?
- What final sentence recharges the opening?
