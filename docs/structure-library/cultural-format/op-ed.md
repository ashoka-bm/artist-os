# Op-Ed

**Entry id**: `op_ed`

**Scope**: A concise opinion article makes a timely, accountable argument for a public audience.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape needs a position, evidence, public relevance, counterargument, and a forceful close.

**Audience promise**: The reader will understand what the writer believes, why it matters now, why the writer can say it, and what should change.

**Audience Hook logic**: Use a news hook, concrete moment, contradiction, anecdote, statistic, or reversal that makes the argument urgent.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News hook or lede | Shows why this argument matters now. | Required |
| Argument / thesis | States or clearly implies the position. | Required |
| Authority | Shows why the speaker is accountable to the claim. | Required |
| Evidence points | Supports the argument with credible material. | Required |
| Counterargument | Acknowledges a strong objection or limitation. | Required when persuasion matters |
| Stakes | Explains who is affected and why the issue matters. | Required |
| Close / call | Ends with consequence, action, warning, or memorable observation. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff should sharpen the argument's public consequence. It may call for action, change perception, or make refusal harder.

**Pacing norms**:

- Make the position clear early.
- Keep evidence pointed rather than exhaustive.
- Treat disagreement seriously.
- Close with pressure, not summary alone.

**Required decisions**:

- What is the arguable thesis?
- Why now?
- Why this speaker?
- What evidence earns trust?
- What counterargument must be faced?
- What should change after the reader accepts the argument?

**Common failure modes**:

- The piece has opinions but no argument.
- The hook is timely but the thesis is generic.
- Evidence is asserted rather than shown.
- The counterargument is weak or caricatured.
- The ending scolds instead of persuading.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What public conversation is this entering?
- What claim can a reasonable reader disagree with?
- What makes the speaker credible or accountable?
- What objection would a fair critic raise?
- What should the reader think, feel, or do at the end?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `argument_map`
- `counterargument_tracker`
- `evidence_stakes_map`
