# Case Study

**Entry id**: `case_study`

**Scope**: A practical or analytical piece examines a specific example to show a problem, intervention, result, and transferable lesson.

**Structure grounding**: Case study situation, challenge, action, result, and lesson convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The piece should make learning concrete through one bounded example rather than general advice or abstract analysis.

**Audience promise**: The reader will understand what happened in a specific case, why choices were made, what changed, and what lesson can transfer.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with the outcome, problem, constraint, surprising detail, or high-stakes choice that makes the case worth studying.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case hook | Shows why this case matters. | Required |
| Situation | Defines the starting context and relevant constraints. | Required |
| Challenge | Names the core problem, risk, or opportunity. | Required |
| Intervention / approach | Explains what was tried and why. | Required |
| Result | Shows measurable, observable, or interpretive outcome. | Required |
| Analysis | Connects action to result without overstating causality. | Required |
| Transferable lesson | States what others can learn or adapt. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The turn often reveals that the real problem was different from the apparent problem, or that the result depended on a specific constraint.

**Pacing norms**:

- Establish the case boundary early.
- Keep chronology clear.
- Distinguish fact, interpretation, and lesson.
- Avoid turning one case into universal proof.

**Required decisions**:

- What makes this case worth examining?
- What were the starting constraints?
- What intervention or choice changed the situation?
- What evidence supports the result?
- What lesson transfers, and what does not?

**Common failure modes**:

- The case becomes promotional success copy.
- The challenge is vague.
- Results are asserted without evidence.
- Causality is overstated.
- The lesson ignores context-specific limits.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What was the before state?
- What problem did the actors think they were solving?
- What actually drove the outcome?
- What evidence is strong enough to include?
- What should the reader adapt cautiously?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `before_after_map`
- `claim_evidence_map`
- `transferability_notes`
