# Subplot Tracker

**Entry id**: `subplot_tracker`

**Scope**: A readable view for tracking a secondary story line through setup, development, intersections, payoff, and status.

**Structure grounding**: Subplot setup, development, intersection, and payoff craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: A secondary plot line affects multiple parts and needs visibility during planning, drafting, or revision.

**Authority mode**: Stewardship View first; CFS when delivered as a subplot tracking document.

**Audience promise**: The subplot will matter to the main work and pay off its reader expectation.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with the subplot's promise: the question, desire, danger, relationship, mystery, or pressure that makes readers track it.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subplot promise | Names the reader expectation opened by the subplot. | Required |
| Owner | Identifies the character, relationship, institution, or pressure carrying it. | Required |
| Setup | Names where and how the subplot opens. | Required |
| Development | Projects how the subplot changes across parts. | Required |
| Intersections | Names where it affects the main arc or other subplots. | Required when relevant |
| Payoff | States how and when the promise resolves, transforms, or remains open. | Required |
| Status | Displays planned, active, resolved, deferred, cut, or at-risk state from Long-Work Stewardship and reviews. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The subplot pays off when its promise changes reader understanding, character choice, main-arc stakes, or thematic pressure.

**Pacing norms**:

- Revisit active subplots often enough that readers remember their promise.
- Do not let a subplot steal authority from the main Beat Plan.
- Use intersections to justify subplot space.
- Mark deferred or intentionally unresolved subplots explicitly.
- Do not mutate Long-Work Stewardship from this view; surface proposed changes for approval.

**Required decisions**:

- What promise does the subplot open?
- Which Long-Work Parts carry it?
- What payoff is required before completion?
- Which continuity rules or proposed updates govern it?

**Common failure modes**:

- The tracker becomes a new authority record.
- A subplot opens without a payoff policy.
- The subplot develops in isolation from the main arc.
- Status hides risk instead of surfacing it.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What would feel broken if this subplot disappeared?
- Where does the subplot intersect the main pressure?
- Does it resolve, transform, defer, or intentionally remain open?
- What checkpoint should catch drift?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `subplot_tracker`
- `open_thread_list`
- `checkpoint_plan`
