# Profile

**Entry id**: `profile`

**Scope**: A reported or observed piece reveals a person, group, place, or subject through scene, detail, context, contradiction, and meaning.

**Structure grounding**: Magazine and newspaper profile convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted form should help the reader understand a subject's character, role, tension, or significance rather than only summarize accomplishments.

**Audience promise**: The reader will encounter the subject as particular and revealing, with enough context to understand why this subject matters.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with a revealing scene, gesture, quote, contradiction, pressure, or detail that embodies the profile's central question.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revealing doorway | Introduces the subject through scene, action, detail, or pressure. | Required |
| Profile question | Frames what the piece is trying to understand about the subject. | Required |
| Context / background | Places the subject in history, field, community, or stakes. | Required |
| Evidence of character | Uses scenes, quotes, choices, artifacts, or contradictions. | Required |
| Complication | Shows tension, limitation, conflict, or unresolved pressure. | Required |
| Larger meaning | Connects the subject to a broader cultural, emotional, or practical significance. | Required |
| Kicker / final image | Leaves the reader with a resonant detail or changed perception. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The turn should complicate the first impression. The payoff should make the opening detail feel diagnostic, ironic, tender, or newly legible.

**Pacing norms**:

- Balance scene and context.
- Avoid front-loading biography.
- Let contradiction shape movement.
- Use quotes and details as evidence, not decoration.

**Required decisions**:

- What question does this profile ask about the subject?
- What first detail best reveals that question?
- What background is essential to meaning?
- What contradiction keeps the profile from becoming promotional?
- What final image or moment should remain?

**Common failure modes**:

- The profile becomes a resume or tribute.
- Scenes are vivid but not interpretive.
- The subject is flattened into a single trait.
- Conflict is avoided for politeness.
- The ending praises instead of revealing.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What would surprise the reader about this subject?
- What pressure reveals character?
- Which detail carries the whole profile in miniature?
- What context changes how the subject is read?
- What should the reader understand that a bio would miss?
