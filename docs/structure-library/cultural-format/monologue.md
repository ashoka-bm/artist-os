# Monologue

**Entry id**: `monologue`

**Scope**: A sustained single-speaker text reveals voice, pressure, self-presentation, and a turn through direct address or overheard speech.

**Structure grounding**: Dramatic and literary monologue craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape is a speech-like literary piece, dramatic monologue, confessional address, performance text, or character voice study.

**Audience promise**: The reader or listener will experience a voice under pressure and discover more than the speaker knowingly states.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with a voice that already wants something: defense, confession, seduction, accusation, explanation, refusal, plea, or performance.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaking situation | Establishes why the speaker is talking now. | Required |
| Implied listener or audience | Creates pressure through who is being addressed, avoided, or imagined. | Required |
| Speaker want | Gives the monologue direction. | Required |
| Voice pattern | Establishes diction, rhythm, avoidance, obsession, or performance style. | Required |
| Escalation or unraveling | Lets the speaker reveal more through pressure, contradiction, or insistence. | Required |
| Turn or exposure | Changes the audience's understanding of the speaker, situation, or stakes. | Required |
| Final address or residue | Leaves the voice altered, exposed, hardened, or unresolved. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff comes when speech betrays, reveals, revises, or intensifies the speaker's position.

**Pacing norms**:

- Keep the speaker's want active.
- Use repetition and digression only when they reveal pressure.
- Let subtext emerge through what the speaker avoids.
- End on a changed relation between speaker and listener.

**Required decisions**:

- Who is speaking?
- Why must they speak now?
- Who is being addressed, even if absent?
- What does the speaker want to make true?
- What leaks out despite the speaker's control?

**Common failure modes**:

- The monologue becomes exposition in one voice.
- The speaker has no pressure or want.
- The voice is colorful but static.
- The piece tells the secret too early.
- The ending summarizes the insight instead of landing the voice.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What is the speaker trying to conceal, prove, or force?
- What contradiction should the audience notice before the speaker does?
- What repeated phrase, rhythm, or avoidance pattern carries the form?
- What final line changes the listener's position?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `character_brief`
- `voice_notes`
- `scene_sheet` when attached to a larger dramatic work
