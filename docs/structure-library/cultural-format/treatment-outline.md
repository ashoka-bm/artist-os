# Treatment Outline

**Entry id**: `treatment_outline`

**Scope**: A prose outline that presents the premise, world/context, main movements, character pressure, and ending promise of a narrative work.

**Structure grounding**: Narrative treatment, premise, act movement, sequence summary, and ending-promise craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The output should read as a coherent narrative plan rather than a tracker, beat sheet, or chapter-by-chapter production artifact.

Continuity details included in a treatment are explanatory summaries. They do not create or revise Long-Work Continuity Rules.

**Audience promise**: The reader will understand the story's shape, stakes, world, and ending direction in prose form.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with the premise, world pressure, character dilemma, or inciting situation that gives the treatment urgency.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logline or premise | Compresses the story promise. | Required |
| World or context | Establishes the setting, situation, or rules needed to understand the story. | Required when relevant |
| Character pressure | Names central wants, conflicts, and stakes. | Required |
| Major movements | Summarizes act, sequence, or chapter-scale progression. | Required |
| Ending promise | Shows the expected destination or unresolved final pressure. | Required |
| Adaptation notes | Names open questions, risks, or choices for later planning. | Optional |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The treatment pays off when the final movement makes the opening premise feel transformed, answered, or productively complicated.

**Pacing norms**:

- Write in clear prose, not spreadsheet detail.
- Keep causal movement visible.
- Avoid overloading the treatment with continuity logs.
- Let uncertainty appear as adaptation notes rather than hidden gaps.

**Required decisions**:

- Is the treatment for planning, pitching, drafting, or review?
- How much ending detail is appropriate?
- Which world or continuity details are necessary for comprehension?
- Which details belong in Stewardship Views instead?

**Common failure modes**:

- The treatment becomes a full outline without readable flow.
- The premise is clear but the ending promise is missing.
- World-building overwhelms story movement.
- It duplicates trackers instead of summarizing them.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What should the reader understand after one pass?
- What can remain unresolved for later?
- Which Long-Work Parts does the treatment summarize?
- What would make the treatment drift from Artist Meaning?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `plot_tracker`
- `chapter_tracker`
- `world_continuity_view`
