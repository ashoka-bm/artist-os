# Literary Scene

**Entry id**: `literary_scene`

**Scope**: A prose scene renders a charged moment through character, place, perception, action, and a turn without needing to complete a whole story.

**Structure grounding**: Literary fiction scene craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape is a prose scene, chapter excerpt, vignette-with-turn, or literary moment that should feel inhabited rather than summarized.

**Audience promise**: The reader will enter a lived moment where attention, pressure, and detail reveal something that changes the felt state.

**Audience Hook logic**: Begin with immediate presence: a charged detail, unstable situation, voice, sensory pressure, or small action that implies larger stakes.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate situation | Places the reader inside a concrete moment. | Required |
| Point of attention | Establishes whose perception, desire, dread, or fixation organizes the scene. | Required |
| Pressure source | Creates friction through event, relationship, memory, place, absence, or contradiction. | Required |
| Embodied development | Lets action, detail, thought, and dialogue deepen or redirect the pressure. | Required |
| Turn of perception or state | Changes what the moment means or what can happen next. | Required |
| Exit image or aftertaste | Leaves the reader with residue rather than explanation. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff is often a change in perception, emotional pressure, relationship state, or symbolic charge rather than plot resolution.

**Pacing norms**:

- Enter at a precise moment, not at general background.
- Let concrete detail carry meaning.
- Keep summary subordinate to present-tense pressure.
- Leave shortly after the scene's state changes.

**Required decisions**:

- Whose perception governs the scene?
- What pressure makes this moment worth entering?
- What changes by the exit?
- What detail can carry theme without explaining it?
- Does the scene need action, silence, memory, dialogue, or image to turn?

**Common failure modes**:

- The scene becomes mood without pressure.
- The piece explains meaning instead of embodying it.
- The scene has beautiful description but no turn.
- Backstory overwhelms the present moment.
- The exit lingers after the aftertaste has landed.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What is the scene's smallest necessary present action?
- What does the reader know or feel at the end that they could not at the start?
- Which sensory or behavioral detail can do the most structural work?
- What should remain implicit?

- `scene_sheet`
- `character_brief`
- `open_threads` for scenes inside longer work
