# Explainer Article

**Entry id**: `explainer_article`

**Scope**: A reader-facing article makes a complex question, system, issue, or event understandable through clear framing, sequence, examples, and implications.

**Structure grounding**: Explainer journalism and educational article convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The reader needs orientation, background, distinctions, causes, mechanics, or implications more than narrative texture or opinion.

**Audience promise**: The reader will leave understanding the subject well enough to explain its basic logic and why it matters.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with the confusion, stakes, misconception, question, or change that makes explanation necessary now.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framing question | Names what needs explaining and why now. | Required |
| Plain-language answer | Gives the reader an early orientation. | Required |
| Background | Supplies context without assuming expertise. | Required |
| Breakdown | Separates the subject into causes, parts, steps, actors, or concepts. | Required |
| Example / scenario | Makes the explanation concrete. | Required unless the subject is already concrete |
| Implications | Shows what changes, matters, or remains contested. | Required |
| Takeaway | Condenses what the reader should retain. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff is comprehension with changed scale: the reader moves from confusion to usable mental model, often with a nuance that revises the initial assumption.

**Pacing norms**:

- Give an early simple answer before adding complexity.
- Introduce terms only when needed.
- Alternate abstraction with concrete examples.
- Keep the explanation ordered by reader understanding, not by research sequence.

**Required decisions**:

- What question is the article answering?
- What does the reader likely misunderstand?
- What background is necessary before the breakdown?
- Which organizing logic fits: parts, timeline, causes, actors, or tradeoffs?
- What practical or interpretive takeaway should remain?

**Common failure modes**:

- The piece explains around the question instead of answering it.
- Background becomes a history dump.
- The structure follows expert logic rather than reader logic.
- Examples arrive too late.
- The takeaway merely repeats the topic.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What would make a smart non-expert feel oriented?
- What is the shortest honest answer?
- Where does the reader need a concrete example?
- What nuance prevents oversimplification?
- What should the reader be able to say after reading?
