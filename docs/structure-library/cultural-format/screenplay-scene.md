# Screenplay Scene

**Entry id**: `screenplay_scene`

**Scope**: A scene written for screen action advances story, reveals character, turns on action or information, and leaves before the energy drains.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape is a screenplay scene, script scene, video scene, or cinematic prose scene that needs visible action, conflict, objective, subtext, or a turn.

**Audience promise**: The scene will show a meaningful change that can be performed, filmed, or imagined as screen action.

**Audience Hook logic**: Enter at the latest moment that still lets the audience understand the scene's pressure. Start with action, behavior, visual contrast, or a charged line rather than setup chatter.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late entry | Starts close to the active pressure. | Required |
| Scene objective | Names what someone wants or what pressure drives the scene. | Required |
| Obstacle or opposition | Creates resistance through character, circumstance, information, or silence. | Required |
| Escalation / exchange | Lets action, dialogue, or image intensify the pressure. | Required |
| Turn | Changes story direction, value, relationship, or information. | Required |
| Early exit | Leaves after the turn has landed and before explanation drains it. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: A scene pays off by changing the state of the story. The change can come through action, information, relationship, mood, decision, or revealed character.

**Pacing norms**:

- Enter late and leave early.
- Keep the scene about one main change.
- Let behavior carry subtext.
- Avoid dialogue that only explains what the audience can see.

**Required decisions**:

- Whose scene is it?
- What does that person want, fear, hide, or resist?
- What blocks them?
- What changes by the end?
- What is the earliest exit after the turn?

**Common failure modes**:

- The scene explains backstory but does not turn.
- Everyone says what they mean.
- The scene starts before pressure begins.
- The scene keeps talking after the change has happened.
- The scene has conflict but no story function.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What visible action can carry the pressure?
- What is the scene's one necessary change?
- What information or value state turns?
- What should stay subtext?
- Where can the scene enter later or exit earlier?
