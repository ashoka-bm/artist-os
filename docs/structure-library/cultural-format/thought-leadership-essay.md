# Thought Leadership Essay

**Entry id**: `thought_leadership_essay`

**Scope**: A public essay advances a point of view about a field, market, practice, or cultural shift.

**Structure grounding**: Platform-era professional essay and opinion leadership pattern.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `platform_pattern_evidence`

**Use when**: The piece should make the writer's perspective feel earned, timely, and useful without becoming a generic article or sales pitch.

**Audience promise**: The reader will understand a meaningful shift, why it matters, and what a credible practitioner thinks it changes.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with a change, contradiction, mistake, observed pattern, or sharp claim that makes the reader question a default assumption.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field signal | Names the shift, tension, or pattern. | Required |
| Point of view | States the writer's read on what the signal means. | Required |
| Authority basis | Shows why the writer can speak from experience, observation, or responsibility. | Required |
| Evidence or examples | Grounds the claim in concrete cases. | Required |
| Implications | Explains what changes if the point of view is true. | Required |
| Reader takeaway | Leaves the reader with a reframe, decision, or practice shift. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff should reframe the reader's professional judgment, not merely summarize the topic.

**Pacing norms**:

- Make the point of view clear early.
- Use examples as proof, not decoration.
- Move from field signal to implications before the piece becomes a memo.
- Avoid abstract authority claims.

**Required decisions**:

- What field, practice, or market is this entering?
- What default belief is being revised?
- What proves the writer has earned the claim?
- What should the reader do or notice differently?

**Common failure modes**:

- The piece has status language but no concrete insight.
- The claim is too broad to be useful.
- The middle becomes brand positioning.
- The ending asks for agreement without giving consequence.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What has the writer seen that the reader may not have noticed yet?
- What does the reader currently believe?
- What pressure makes the view timely?
- What practical or interpretive shift should remain?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `argument_map`
- `evidence_stakes_map`
- `promise_payoff`
