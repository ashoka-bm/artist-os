# Novel Outline

**Entry id**: `novel_outline`

**Scope**: A long-fiction planning artifact that turns a premise into a book-scale shape with genre promise, act movement, major character pressure, and ending direction.

**Structure grounding**: Long-form fiction outline, act structure, genre contract, and chapter-brief craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted written output is a novel, novella, long serial, or book-length fiction plan that needs enough structure to support later chapters or scenes.

**Audience promise**: The plan will make the story coherent at book scale without pretending every scene is already solved.

**Audience Hook logic**: Start with the central story pressure: a premise, contradiction, danger, desire, mystery, or promise that makes the long work worth following.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premise | States the core story situation and pressure. | Required |
| Genre promise | Names what the reader expects from the form and subgenre. | Required |
| Thematic spine | Captures the meaning pressure the story keeps testing. | Required |
| Act movement | Shows major turns, escalation, reversal, crisis, climax, and resolution direction. | Required |
| Character pressure | Names planned wants, fears, contradictions, and change paths without converting them into approved continuity. | Required |
| Chapter or sequence map | Projects planned cumulative parts from Text Medium Plan and Long-Work `part_plan` when available. | Required |
| Open risks | Names weak spots, unresolved questions, and continuity risks. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The outline pays off by showing how the ending direction answers, reverses, or transforms the initial promise.

**Pacing norms**:

- Keep the act movement visible before adding dense detail.
- Do not solve every scene before the major turns are clear.
- Keep genre promise and emotional arc aligned.
- Surface unknowns instead of inventing false certainty.

**Required decisions**:

- What genre promise governs reader expectation?
- What ending direction is known enough to plan toward?
- What major turns must the Long-Work Stewardship part plan preserve?
- Which open risks need checkpoints before drafting?

**Common failure modes**:

- The outline becomes a story bible or continuity authority.
- The chapter map is detailed but the act movement is unclear.
- Genre promise is named but not structurally honored.
- Character arcs are listed without pressure or choices.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What must be known before chapter drafting is safe?
- Which parts can remain provisional?
- What should trigger a Long-Work Checkpoint?
- What should become a continuity rule only after approval?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `plot_tracker`
- `chapter_tracker`
- `open_thread_list`
- `character_continuity_view`
