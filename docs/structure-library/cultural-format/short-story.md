# Short Story

**Entry id**: `short_story`

**Scope**: A compact fictional prose work creates a complete narrative pressure, image, turn, or emotional residue within limited space.

**Structure grounding**: Short fiction craft convention.

**Grounding status**: `researched`

**Grounding tier**: `craft_authority`

**Use when**: The accepted shape needs character, situation, tension, escalation or contrast, a decisive moment, and an ending that feels complete even when open.

**Audience promise**: The reader will experience a whole story, not a scene fragment, summary, premise, or chapter opening.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open near pressure: a charged image, decision, desire, disruption, contradiction, or voice that makes the reader trust the story's urgency.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening pressure | Pulls the reader into a specific situation, voice, or disturbance. | Required |
| Character desire or lack | Gives the story a human or symbolic engine. | Required |
| Situation / world | Establishes enough context for the pressure to matter. | Required |
| Escalation or development | Changes the pressure rather than merely extending it. | Required |
| Turn or climax | Forces recognition, choice, reversal, exposure, or loss. | Required |
| Ending / residue | Gives the story completion, release, wound, echo, or unresolved force. | Required |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The ending should make the opening feel transformed by what happened between them.

**Pacing norms**:

- Enter close to the pressure.
- Use few characters and few locations unless length supports more.
- Let details do structural work.
- Give the ending consequence, even when it stays open.

**Required decisions**:

- Who or what carries the central pressure?
- What changes between the first and last paragraph?
- What can be omitted because the short form cannot carry it?
- Does the ending resolve, reveal, wound, echo, or withhold?
- What image or action should hold the residue?

**Common failure modes**:

- The piece is a premise, vignette, or mood without a turn.
- Too many characters or subplots dilute pressure.
- The climax happens offstage or in summary.
- The ending explains the story's meaning.
- The story starts too early.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What is the smallest complete story here?
- What pressure is present in the opening?
- What change must the reader feel by the end?
- Which detail can carry more than one job?
- What should remain unsaid?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `plot_tracker`
- `character_brief`
- `open_threads` for linked stories or longer short fiction
