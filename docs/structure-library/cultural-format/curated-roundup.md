# Curated Roundup

**Entry id**: `curated_roundup`

**Scope**: A grouped selection of items helps the reader notice, compare, and act on material they would not process as clearly alone.

**Structure grounding**: Newsletter, blog roundup, and curated link/list pattern.

**Grounding status**: `provisional`

**Grounding tier**: `platform_pattern_evidence`

**Use when**: The value is not original argument alone, but selection, grouping, annotation, and synthesis.

**Audience promise**: The reader will get a useful filter: what matters, why it matters, and how the items relate.

**Audience Hook logic**: Open with the selection logic, theme, urgency, or reader benefit that makes the roundup more than a pile of links.

**Expected parts**:

| Part | Function | Default policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curation frame | Explains the theme, filter, or reason for the collection. | Required |
| Grouped items | Presents selected items in a legible order. | Required |
| Annotation | Says why each item matters. | Required |
| Pattern synthesis | Names the larger pattern across items. | Required when more than a simple list |
| Next action | Gives the reader a starting point, priority, or use case. | Mergeable |

**Turn or payoff behavior**: The payoff is synthesis: the reader sees a pattern or priority they would not get from the items alone.

**Pacing norms**:

- State the filter before the list.
- Keep annotations short but opinionated.
- Group items by reader need, not merely source order.
- End by reducing choice, not expanding it.

**Required decisions**:

- What qualifies something for inclusion?
- Are items ranked, grouped, chronological, or thematic?
- What does each annotation add beyond description?
- What pattern should the reader see by the end?

**Common failure modes**:

- The roundup is an unfiltered dump.
- Every item gets the same generic note.
- The order hides the intended pattern.
- The close adds more material instead of clarifying priority.

**Adaptation questions**:

- What does the reader need a filter for?
- What is the smallest useful set?
- What grouping makes the material easiest to use?
- Which item should the reader start with?

**Recommended Stewardship Views**:

- `section_outline`
- `evidence_stakes_map`
- `decision_tree`
