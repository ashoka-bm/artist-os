## What It Is

This transcript breaks down ways to open a speech so the audience pays attention quickly. It is useful for video because short-form videos have the same first-problem: the viewer must know why to keep watching.

## Hook Types

- **Question hook:** asks something the audience automatically tries to answer.
- **Surprising statement:** uses a statistic, fact, or claim that creates doubt or curiosity.
- **Story-in-the-moment:** starts inside a scene instead of giving long background.
- **Big promise:** tells the audience what they will gain, not merely what the video will cover.
- **Visual action hook:** does something unexpected before or as the first line lands.

## Artist OS Mapping

These are hook-entry tactics. They should not become standalone story templates unless they include a full turn and payoff.

They belong in direction notes and can be selected during Video Medium Plan or script planning after the Story Template is known.

## Draft Rule

Every short video should have a hook posture, but the hook posture must serve the Story Template. A surprising statement can open Myth -> Reality -> Move; a question can open Misread Signal -> Reframe -> Move; a visual action hook can open a cinematic or sketch format.

## Risks

- A hook gets optimized for attention but does not connect to payoff.
- The opening promises a gain the story does not deliver.
- A visual action hook becomes random spectacle.
- The video starts with context before creating curiosity.
