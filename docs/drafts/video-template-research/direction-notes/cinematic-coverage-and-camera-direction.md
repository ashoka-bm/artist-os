# Direction Notes: Cinematic Coverage And Camera Direction

Status: research draft.

These notes guide storyboard and provider-export decisions after the story beat and format are known. They are not a Story Template. They help decide how the camera should serve the moment.

## Core Rule

Choose coverage from the scene's emotional job:

- What must the viewer understand?
- What must the viewer feel?
- What should be withheld, revealed, clarified, or landed?

Do not pick angles, shot sizes, or camera movement as decoration. Every camera choice should serve tone, pacing, stakes, or clarity.

## Coverage Economy

- Use the fewest shots that tell the scene well.
- Merge adjacent actions into one flowing shot when the action can stay legible.
- Avoid splitting one continuous movement into multiple artificial shots.
- Treat six shots as a practical upper bound for a short generated scene unless a user explicitly chooses more.

## Scale Progression

Use shot size as emotional pressure:

- extreme wide or wide: geography, scale, subject vs world;
- full shot: body action, posture, movement;
- medium: neutral coverage, dialogue, two-person blocking;
- close-up: emotion, reaction, interior state;
- extreme close-up: peak intensity, decisive detail, held beat.

Start looser when the viewer needs context. Push tighter as emotion, danger, intimacy, or realization increases. Avoid staying at the same shot size for too many consecutive cuts.

## Angle Choice

Use angle for meaning:

- front angle: confrontation, vulnerability, direct address, emotional truth;
- side angle: motion, profile drama, parallel action;
- over-the-shoulder: relationship, tension, point of view between two subjects;
- behind: mystery, reveal, following what the subject sees;
- low angle: power, threat, scale, triumph;
- high angle: vulnerability, surveillance, isolation, smallness;
- canted angle: unease, wrongness, psychological disturbance;
- wide and slightly high: geography, environment, subject against scale.

Change angle meaningfully across cuts so the edit feels motivated instead of jumpy.

## Camera Movement

Use one primary camera movement per shot:

- push in: dread, realization, pressure, intimacy;
- pull out: isolation, loss, distance, aftermath;
- track or follow: momentum, pursuit, movement with a subject;
- pan: reveal, lateral following, space discovery;
- tilt: scale reveal or power shift;
- handheld: panic, immediacy, documentary energy;
- locked frame: stillness, dread, patience, weight.

Avoid stacking movements in one shot unless the chosen production route can handle it.

## Tension Coverage

Tension often improves when the camera withholds information:

- hold on the reacting face instead of showing the threat immediately;
- let sound imply danger before the image confirms it;
- use a slow push-in on a face when dread is growing;
- cut to a detail only when the detail changes the viewer's understanding;
- hold the frame briefly after a line or action when the silence is the point.

## Directorial Pushback

If a requested camera choice weakens the emotional beat, flag it once with a short reason and offer the requested version anyway. The artist makes the final call.

Example: a high angle can make a character feel small. If the beat needs power or threat, recommend a lower angle, then proceed with the artist's preference if they keep it.

## Flow Placement

- Beat Plan defines the emotional movement.
- Video Medium Plan can carry camera posture, shot scale progression, and transition intent when these affect the output.
- Storyboard Shots translate the direction into specific shot choices.
- Provider Export adapts the same choices to the target platform's syntax and limitations.
