# Direction Notes: Hook Entry Patterns

Status: research draft.

Hooks are entry tactics. They create attention, but they do not replace the story template.

## Five Hook Families

- **Question:** asks something the viewer starts answering internally.
- **Surprising statement:** creates productive doubt with a bold claim, statistic, or contradiction.
- **Story-in-the-moment:** drops the viewer into a concrete scene without long setup.
- **Big promise:** states what the viewer will gain by staying.
- **Visual action:** begins with an unexpected action, object, silence, or image.

## Placement In The Flow

Hook posture should be chosen after the Story Template is known and before scripting or storyboard detail hardens.

Recommended placement:

1. Beat Plan owns the story movement.
2. Medium Output Shape Recommendation confirms the output shape.
3. Video Medium Plan selects hook posture as part of format translation.
4. Provider Export renders the hook in platform-specific form.

## Hook/Payoff Rule

The hook must point toward the payoff. If the payoff is a practical move, the hook should create a question, misconception, or promise that the move resolves. If the payoff is emotional, the hook should create a visible tension the ending transforms.

## Bad Hook Signs

- It is interesting but unrelated to the ending.
- It creates a promise the video does not satisfy.
- It delays the story with generic context.
- It asks a question the video never answers.
- It shows visual action that never becomes meaningful.
