# Full-Story Walkthrough: 90s Sitcom AI Replacement

Status: research draft.

Purpose: test `narrative_depth = full_story` against the current Video Journey guidance without editing canonical docs or schemas.

## Source Concept

A 90s sitcom version of the creator enters three chronological scenes. In each scene, the creator is happy about finishing a new career credential: copywriting, coding, then video editing / film school. Each time, it becomes clear that AI threatens or replaces that field. The final scene should include a fourth-wall nod that makes the video editor replacement question feel uncomfortably current.

## Walkthrough Packet

### Narrative Depth

`full_story`

Rationale: the video is not just a sequence of gags. It needs a repeated desire, escalating frustration, a final turn, and a payoff. The audience movement is: "I can outrun AI by learning the next skill" -> "the target keeps moving" -> "the durable move is creative judgment, taste, and direction, not just the latest credential."

### Format Intent

Short social intro video with a sitcom sketch format.

### Accepted Output Shape

Primary shape: `short_social_video`

Format modifiers:

- `short_film`
- `single_scene`, repeated as three parallel sitcom scenes

Rationale: the piece is meant to introduce a creator idea quickly, but it uses a narrative sketch rather than a talking-head explainer.

### Required Story Structure / Story Movement

Recommended draft Story Template: `five_line_emotional_story`

Adapted movement:

1. Situation: a 90s sitcom world where the creator keeps chasing stable creative/technical careers.
2. Desire: the creator wants security, relevance, and a hard-earned professional identity.
3. Conflict: every completed credential arrives just as AI threatens that field.
4. Change: the repeated pattern makes the career-by-career solution collapse.
5. Result: the final fourth-wall beat reframes the issue: the threat is not one job title; it is the need to direct the tools with taste, judgment, and a point of view.

Secondary draft pattern: `problem_failed_fix_better_move`

- Problem: AI makes skill-based career identity feel unstable.
- Failed fix: keep switching to the next supposedly safer credential.
- Better move: develop creative direction, judgment, storytelling, and tool orchestration.

### Hook Posture

Primary hook posture: `story-in-the-moment`

Opening hook: the creator bursts through the sitcom door holding a copywriting degree, visibly proud, before the room punctures the victory.

Secondary hook posture: `surprising statement`

Underlying promise: "Every time I learned the safe skill, AI got there first."

### Payoff

The payoff should not be only "AI replaces everyone." It should land on the uncomfortable pattern:

- the creator keeps treating the next credential as safety,
- AI keeps turning that safety into uncertainty,
- the final fourth-wall nod makes the audience question whether the video itself is part of the replacement pattern.

Possible final beat: the creator celebrates finishing film school and becoming a video editor, then notices the camera/editorial timing and asks whether the editor is still in the room. The scene holds a beat too long, then fades like a sitcom ending.

### Video Scenes

#### Scene 1: Copywriting Degree

Local purpose: establish the repeated structure and first reversal.

Setting: 90s sitcom apartment living room.

Beat group:

- pride / arrival,
- professional identity,
- sudden replacement pressure,
- sitcom fade-out.

Action:

The creator enters through the front door holding a copywriting degree, grinning. The roommate or TV reveals that copywriters are being replaced by AI. The creator's smile drops. Freeze or awkward laugh-track pause, then fade to black.

#### Scene 2: Coding Degree

Local purpose: escalate the pattern. The creator tried to adapt, but the same force moved.

Setting: same apartment, later chronological beat, with a slightly changed outfit or prop.

Beat group:

- renewed confidence,
- "I solved it this time",
- second reversal,
- sharper frustration.

Action:

The creator enters again, now proud of a coding credential. The roommate reacts as if this is familiar. News, a laptop, or a roommate line reveals that coders are also being displaced by AI. The creator freezes in a more self-aware version of the first disappointment. Fade to black.

#### Scene 3: Video Editing / Film School

Local purpose: deliver the turn and fourth-wall payoff.

Setting: same apartment, later again, with film school / editing props.

Beat group:

- third attempt,
- fragile hope,
- current-tense threat,
- fourth-wall nod.

Action:

The creator enters with film school / video editing proof. Before anyone can fully puncture the moment, the creator senses the pattern. A line, camera move, or editing glitch suggests that video editors may be next. The creator looks into the camera or toward the edit itself, making the fourth-wall question visible. Fade to black.

## Storyboard Scope

Recommended scope: 9 to 12 Storyboard Shots across 3 Video Scenes.

Possible shot distribution:

- Scene 1: 3 to 4 shots.
- Scene 2: 3 to 4 shots.
- Scene 3: 3 to 4 shots.

Storyboard priorities:

- preserve the repeated entry structure,
- make each credential visually legible,
- show escalating emotional reaction,
- vary details enough that each repetition feels intentional,
- reserve the most direct camera acknowledgment for the final scene.

Do not generate storyboard stills or clips without explicit Generation Approval.

## Audio / Text Posture

Video Audio Posture: `dialogue-led`, with possible sitcom sound-design cues.

Text Journey likely required for:

- short dialogue lines,
- fourth-wall line,
- any on-screen news or TV text,
- optional sitcom-style title card or act break text.

Sound Journey is not required for this walkthrough unless music or sound design becomes a first-class deliverable. Raw provider clips should not assume music or subtitles.

## Reference Needs

Promote visual references before storyboard lock:

- creator identity / 90s version of creator,
- 90s sitcom apartment set,
- recurring roommate or supporting character if used,
- three credential props: copywriting degree, coding certificate, film school / video editing proof,
- outfit or styling progression across the three scenes,
- optional TV, laptop, or editing monitor used as AI-news delivery device.

Continuity-critical state changes:

- credential changes by scene,
- outfit or time-passage signal,
- the creator's emotional state escalates from proud to anxious to self-aware.

Character Template and Visual Reference Sheet Plans are recommended if the creator likeness and roommate repeat across all scenes.

## Provider Preferences As Non-Binding Notes

Provider preference notes are downstream only:

- possible Seedance 2 execution with one generated clip per scene,
- possible Higgsfield / visual-style reference for the 90s sitcom look,
- keep provider clips short if the platform has duration limits,
- use the last frame of a prior sequence only after storyboard approval if continuity chaining is needed,
- consider 24fps, concrete camera style, concrete lighting, no subtitles, no music, and film grain only during provider export.

These notes must not replace Story Structure, Beat Plan, Video Scenes, or Storyboard Shots.

## Risks / Gaps In Current Framework

- Full-story routing works, but this example shows that repeated-scene comedy needs an explicit "escalating repetition" note. The current Story Template library does not yet have a dedicated repeated-escalation sketch template.
- `narrative_depth` is durable now. The remaining gap is the lack of a dedicated repeated-escalation sketch Story Template.
- The final meaning is still open. It could be bleak replacement anxiety, creator resilience, or a sharper argument about taste and direction. Artist Meaning should resolve that before Beat Plan approval.
- Text Journey needs to own dialogue. The Video Medium Plan should only carry dialogue refs, timing, and placement.
- Provider duration limits may push the piece into three separate clips, but that is a Production Route concern after storyboard approval.

## Recommendations Before Promotion

1. Keep this as `full_story`, not `micro_journey`; the repeated credential failures create a real story turn.
2. Use `five_line_emotional_story` as the primary Story Template for now.
3. Add a future draft Story Template candidate for repeated escalation sketches if more sitcom/comedy examples arrive.
4. During a real run, ask the artist to confirm the final meaning: AI anxiety, reinvention, or "taste and direction are the durable skill."
5. Test whether 9 to 12 Storyboard Shots are enough before adding a repeated-escalation sketch template or any future format-template refs.
